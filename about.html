Mission & Problem Statement

The First Responder Should Be Robotic

In industrial emergencies, chemical leaks, and structural collapses, human personnel are routinely sent in to evaluate the situation before the true extent of the hazard is understood. ARGUS transforms that paradigm.

The Static Sensor Dilemma

Industrial facilities rely heavily on stationary gas detectors and ceiling-mounted CCTV cameras. While useful for ambient background monitoring, they cannot inspect ruptured valves behind pallet racks, navigate narrow catwalks, or confirm whether an alarm is a localized anomaly or an escalating catastrophe.

The Cost of Unverified Entry

Sending first responders or plant technicians into an unverified zone wearing standard PPE carries immense risk. Exposure to toxic fumes, secondary explosions from ignition sources, or unexpected structural instability puts human lives on the line for basic reconnaissance.

Deployment Domains

Where ARGUS Operates

Built to inspect rugged, volatile, and constrained spaces.

Chemical Warehouses

Continuous monitoring of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and combustible solvents stored in sealed drums.

Manufacturing Plants

Routine night inspections and off-shift patrolling around high-voltage machinery and furnace perimeters.

Confined Industrial Ducts

Inspection of basement service corridors, cable trenches, and ventilation rooms too hazardous for routine entry.

Engineered for Impact

Smart India Hackathon 2024 Project

ARGUS was designed and prototyped to address real-world industrial disaster management. By combining affordable commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) embedded hardware with state-of-the-art computer vision and fail-safe safety logic, ARGUS offers a practical, deployable solution rather than a theoretical concept.
